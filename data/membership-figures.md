# AOM: Public Figures

> Compiled from public AOM materials; verify against the current AOM annual report before quoting exact numbers to the Board. Marked approximate where memory-compiled.

- **Members:** ~18,000-20,000, from ~120 countries (roughly half outside the US)
- **Divisions and interest groups:** 26 (MC, TIM, ODC, STR, OB, ENT, and others)
- **Journals owned by AOM:** Academy of Management Journal (AMJ), Academy of Management Review (AMR), Academy of Management Annals, Academy of Management Perspectives (AMP), Academy of Management Discoveries (AMD), Academy of Management Learning & Education (AMLE). Note: AOM owns its journals, unlike fields whose flagship titles sit with commercial publishers. This is unusual strategic leverage (see `documents/lariviere-publisher-oligopoly.md`).
- **Annual meeting:** ~10,000 attendees in a typical year; the field's central convening event
- **Structural position:** AOM does not accredit schools (AACSB does), does not set tenure standards (universities do), and does not control rankings (FT, UTD, US News do). Its assets: convening power, journal ownership, brand legitimacy, and the member network.

## Questions this raises for the diagnosis

1. If AOM owns the journals, who is the constraint on access, format, and translation products: the publisher contract, the editors, or the incentives of authors?
2. What share of members are at non-US schools already operating under impact-assessment regimes (UK REF and similar)? These members live under a different scorecard; they are a natural experiment inside the membership.
3. What does the association's revenue depend on (membership dues, meeting registration, journal licensing)? Any recommendation that threatens a revenue pillar needs a transition path.
