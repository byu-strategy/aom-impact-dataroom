# Grand Challenges Through Management Research

**Full citation:** George, G., Howard-Grenville, J., Joshi, A., & Tihanyi, L. 2016. Understanding and tackling societal grand challenges through management research. *Academy of Management Journal*, 59(6): 1880–1895. DOI: 10.5465/amj.2016.4007

**Why it's in this data room:** This is the field's own aspirational redirection, written by AMJ's editorial team as the capstone to a Special Research Forum: a programmatic statement that management research can (and should) address society's biggest problems without sacrificing publishability in the field's top journal. It grounds the engagement's innovation-opportunities theme: grand-challenge research is an existing, board-adjacent proof of concept for pairing rigor with real-world impact.

## Core Argument

George and colleagues argue that global problems (climate change, poverty, aging societies, inequality) are fundamentally organizational and managerial problems as much as scientific ones, and that management scholars are therefore distinctively positioned to contribute. Borrowing the "grand challenge" construct from Hilbert's 1900 mathematics agenda and the UN Sustainable Development Goals, they define grand challenges as barrier-removal problems requiring coordinated, multi-stakeholder effort. Reviewing 14 accepted articles (from more than 130 submissions) to AMJ's Special Research Forum, they show management theory illuminating microfinance, disaster response, conflict minerals, health care, and workforce aging. They then develop a framework (articulation and participation, multilevel actions, and outcomes, shaped by actor aspirations, societal barriers, organizational constraints, institutional contexts, coordinating architectures, and reinforcing mechanisms) with representative empirical questions, closing with an explicit call to action for scholars and educators.

## Key Evidence & Quotes

- "'Grand challenges' are formulations of global problems that can be plausibly addressed through coordinated and collaborative effort." (p. 1880)
- "This is an opportune moment for management scholars to join the debate and turn research into actionable insights to frame and tackle some of the biggest challenges that we face in our global community." (p. 1880)
- They "define a 'grand challenge' as specific critical barrier(s) that, if removed, would help solve an important societal problem with a high likelihood of global impact through widespread implementation." (p. 1881)
- "These studies collectively highlight that management research can serve a complementary function to corporate, social, and multilateral initiatives by helping better understand the problems, and, indeed, by providing an organizational perspective to convert stubborn societal problems into tractable managerial challenges." (p. 1887)
- "Management scholars are conflicted, but in a different way—we recognize and aspire to engage in solving global problems, but feel that these questions, data, and contexts are structurally inaccessible to us. This Special Research Forum highlights that tackling global problems does not imply that we cannot publish our results in leading journals." (p. 1892)
- "Management scholars, thus, are uniquely positioned to address GCs toward a more socially inclusive society by tackling fundamental individual, behavioral, organizational, and institutional challenges that are omnipresent in the formulation, articulation, coordination, and implementation of these GCs." (p. 1893)
- "As scholars and educators, there is a moral imperative that we act to guide business leaders, employees, and stakeholders with systematic, unbiased, and empirically robust evidence on mechanisms with which to tackle the persistent, but tractable, global problems confounding us." (p. 1893)

## Relevance to the Engagement

The paper hands the consulting team a ready-made strategic template AOM can scale. Special research forums demonstrably redirect scholarly attention (130+ submissions from a single editorial call), suggesting AOM could institutionalize grand-challenge research products: recurring SDG-aligned forums across its journals, problem-first conference tracks, and practitioner-accessible translations of forum findings. The authors' candor about scholars feeling that impactful "questions, data, and contexts" are structurally inaccessible points directly at incentive redesign: data-access partnerships, credit for team-based problem-driven work, and tenure signals that reward societal outcomes alongside citations. Their framework (Table 2's empirical questions) could seed a portfolio of fundable, board-endorsed research programs.

## Both/And Note

Nothing here trades rigor for relevance: the authors explicitly demand "systematic, unbiased, and empirically robust evidence" and showcase impact work published in the field's most rigorous outlet. The paper is the strongest available evidence that rigor and societal impact are complements: the both/and premise of this engagement, stated by AMJ's own editors.
