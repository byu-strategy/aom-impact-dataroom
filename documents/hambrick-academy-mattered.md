# Hambrick: What If the Academy Actually Mattered?

**Full citation:** Hambrick, D. C. 1994. 1993 Presidential Address: What if the Academy actually mattered? *Academy of Management Review*, 19(1): 11–16. DOI: 10.2307/258833.

**Why it's in this data room:** This is the engagement question asked from the AOM podium itself, by a sitting Academy president, more than thirty years ago. The Board's current mandate is Hambrick's address operationalized: proof that the impact question is a longstanding insider concern, not an external attack on the field.

## Core Argument

Hambrick borrows the device of *It's A Wonderful Life* and inverts it. Instead of showing the Academy how much the world would have missed without it, he imagines a counterfactual "Society for Administrative Science" founded in 1949 that came to genuinely matter: a Nobel category in administrative science, a widely adopted code of ethics, a Presidential advisory council, invitations from the new Polish government. The discomfort is that the world might have done very well without the actual Academy. Hambrick attributes the gap not to outside hostility but to the Academy's own "minimalist ethos" and self-referential publication cycle. He proposes concrete remedies (upgrading the *Academy of Management Executive*, joint division-practitioner conferences, a major award for scholarly contribution to practice judged on ten years of sustained impact, and an annual meeting outside North America) and closes by calling the field to enter practical affairs without being co-opted.

## Key Evidence & Quotes

- "The first thing we find—and perhaps most unsettling of all—is that there is another organization in our place. For, unlike individuals, institutions are replaceable." (p. 12)
- "Members of the Academy of Management, that is what might have been. We might have mattered." (p. 13)
- "Rather, we simply seem to have a minimalist ethos: minimal programming, minimal dues, minimal staff, minimal innovation, minimal visibility, minimal impact." (p. 13)
- "Each August, we come to talk with each other; during the rest of the year we read each others' papers in our journals and write our own papers so that we may, in turn, have an audience the following August: an incestuous, closed loop." (p. 13)
- "It is time for us to break out of our closed loop. It is time for us to matter." (p. 13)
- "Right now, the major debates regarding business and management are framed almost entirely by lawyers and economists." (p. 15)
- "Any academic field that is a reluctant wallflower will wilt. Any academic field that exists to satisfy itself and only its own interests will soon have few resources." (p. 16)
- "Without being co-opted, it must strive for influence and impact. That is our challenge. We should matter. We must matter." (p. 16)

## Relevance to the Engagement

Hambrick's diagnosis and prescriptions map directly onto AOM's present strategy levers. His four 1993 proposals (a practitioner-facing flagship publication, division-level joint ventures with practitioner organizations, an impact-focused award judged on ten-year sustained influence, and internationalized convening) are early versions of what the Board can now revisit at scale: communication channels built for managers and policymakers, convening power deployed jointly with practice communities, and incentive architecture that honors contribution to practice alongside contribution to theory. His observation that management debates are framed by lawyers and economists is a market-positioning insight: the demand for management expertise exists, and the Academy has under-supplied it. The consulting team can treat this address as a thirty-year baseline against which to measure what has and has not changed.

## Both/And Note

Hambrick is explicitly not anti-rigor. He affirms that observing, analyzing, critiquing, and disseminating "is important work, and we should never take our eyes off it" (p. 16), and his proposed award privileges sustained, genuine impact over fads and quick fixes. The address models the both/and stance: keep the scholarship, add the translation, convening, and outreach that let it matter.
