# RRBM: A Vision of Responsible Research in Business and Management

**Source:** Responsible Research in Business and Management (RRBM), position paper "A Vision of Responsible Research in Business and Management: Striving for Useful and Credible Knowledge" (2017, revised since). White paper, no DOI, https://rrbm.network. Co-founded by 24 senior scholars including Anne Tsui (AOM President, 2011–12); endorsed by hundreds of scholars, senior editors, deans, and partner institutions including AACSB.

**Why it's in this data room:** proof that reform is an *insider* movement, not an outside attack, the field's own presidents, editors, and deans have already signed a document saying the incentive system needs to change. Any strategy the Board adopts starts with this coalition already in place.

## Core Argument

Business-school research has become highly *credible* (rigorous methods, peer review) without being reliably *useful*, and occasionally neither, when publication incentives reward novelty and quantity over truth and relevance. RRBM's vision statement for 2030: a future in which business schools are "admired for their contributions to societal well-being," which requires research that is **both credible AND useful**, the same both/and this engagement is built on.

## The Seven Principles (paraphrased)

1. **Service to society**, research as a public good, aimed at problems important to business and society
2. **Stakeholder involvement**, engaging those affected (managers, employees, communities) in the research process
3. **Impact on stakeholders**, valuing research that changes practice, policy, or well-being
4. **Valuing both basic and applied contributions**, explicitly both/and, not applied-only
5. **Sound methodology**, rigor is a principle of *responsible* research, not a casualty of it
6. **Plurality and multidisciplinary collaboration**, many methods, fields, and questions
7. **Broad dissemination**, communicating findings beyond paywalled journals to those who can use them

## Relevance to the Engagement

RRBM is the closest thing to an existing implementation roadmap in the data room, and its limits are diagnostic. Eight-plus years in: broad endorsement, pilot journals with badges and special issues, dean signatories, yet the interviews in this data room (editor, dean, PhD student) describe the *same equilibrium* RRBM set out to change. Endorsement has not become scorecard change. The strategic question for the Board is therefore not "what should the field value?" (RRBM answered that, with signatures) but "what mechanism converts stated values into what tenure committees, rankings, and editors actually count?", the coordination problem every interview points at.

## Both/And Note

RRBM principle 5 (sound methodology) and principle 4 (basic AND applied) make it explicit: this movement does not trade rigor for relevance. It is the field's own statement that the sweet spot is both.
