# Heckman & Moktan: The Tyranny of the Top Five

**Full citation:** Heckman, J. J., & Moktan, S. 2020. Publishing and Promotion in Economics: The Tyranny of the Top Five. *Journal of Economic Literature* 58(2): 419-470. doi:10.1257/jel.20191574.

**Why it's in this data room:** The empirical companion to Akerlof (same JEL symposium): a Nobel laureate quantifying how a handful of journals became the tenure currency of an entire discipline, and what that concentration does to careers, ideas, and gatekeeping power. It grounds the engagement's promotion-and-tenure and monopoly themes with data rather than opinion.

## Core Argument

Analyzing job histories of tenure-track economists hired by the top 35 US economics departments (1996-2010) plus a survey of junior faculty, the authors show that publication in the "top five" (T5) journals dominates tenure outcomes: three T5 articles are associated with a 310 percent increase in the rate of receiving tenure relative to candidates with the same number of publications placed elsewhere. Yet the T5 filter is unreliable as a quality screen (many of the most influential papers appear outside it), acceptance rates fell from 15 to 6 percent while page space stayed flat, editorial power is concentrated and long-tenured with measurable insider-network effects, and the whole apparatus channels young scholars toward safe, publishable work. Their conclusion: the discipline should de-emphasize the T5 in tenure decisions, return to actually reading candidates' work, and consider open-source publication formats that break editorial monopolies.

## Key Evidence & Quotes

- "Pursuit of T5 publications has become the obsession of the next generation of economists. However, the T5 screen is far from reliable. A substantial share of influential publications appear in non-T5 outlets. Reliance on the T5 to screen talent incentivizes careerism over creativity." (p. 419)
- "For many young economists, if a paper on any topic cannot be published in a T5 outlet, the topic is not worth pursuing." (p. 420)
- "publishing three T5 articles is associated with a 310 percent increase in the rate of receiving tenure, compared to candidates with similar levels of publications who do not place any in the T5." (p. 421)
- "The acceptance rates at T5 journals declined from 15 percent in 1980 to 6 percent in 2012." (p. 420, citing Card and DellaVigna 2013)
- "many of the most important papers published in the past 50 years have been too innovative to survive the T5 gauntlet." (p. 421)
- "Reliance on the T5 centralizes power to shape the profession into the hands of a select group of editors." (p. 462)
- "an overemphasis on T5 publications perversely incentivizes scholars to pursue follow-up and replication work at the expense of creative pioneering research... you get what you incentivize." (p. 462)
- "academic economics risks becoming (or remaining) a group of top five plodders putting one foot in front of the other." (p. 464)

## The Management Analog

Replace "T5" with the A-journal lists that govern business schools (FT50, UTD24, school-specific A-lists) and every mechanism transfers: tenure decided by counts in listed journals, topic choice filtered by list-acceptability before research begins, falling acceptance rates raising the compliance premium, and long-tenured editorial networks concentrating gatekeeping power. The PhD-student interview in this data room is a first-person account of exactly the incentive structure Heckman and Moktan measure. Two of their remedies map directly onto AOM's option space: departmental peer review that reads work instead of counting placements (a norm AOM could legitimate), and open-source publication formats (AOM owns its journals, so the arXiv/PLOS-style move is unusually available to it).

## Both/And Note

The authors grant the case for a quality signal in a large, specialized profession; their objection is that this particular proxy is unreliable and its side effects severe. The paper argues for better quality judgment (closer reading, more valid signals), not lower standards, which is exactly the both/and posture of this engagement.
