# Engagement Brief: AOM's Research Impact Play

> **Workshop framing note:** This is a teaching case. Imagine the engagement is real; the data and documents are. Public sources throughout; interview notes are illustrative composites, not real individuals.

## The Client

The Academy of Management: the world's largest professional association for management scholars, roughly 18,000 members across ~120 countries, 26 divisions and interest groups, owner of the field's flagship journals (AMJ, AMR, Annals, AMP, AMD, AMLE) and of the annual meeting you are attending right now.

## The State of Play (Why You Are Here Now)

Two things happened in the last ten weeks. Together they are the reason this engagement exists.

**1. The field published its answer.** On 26 May 2026, a task force of ten scholarly and accrediting organizations released *A Framework for Research Impact: Insights, Pathways, and Calls to Action*. It argues that research impact must be understood beyond citations, that incentive systems are the binding constraint, and that no organization can fix this alone. AOM was a lead partner. **Three consecutive AOM presidents sat on the task force**: Sharon Alvarez, Peter Bamberger, and current President Tammy L. Madsen. The full report is in your data room.

**2. The accreditor made it binding.** On 1 July 2026, four weeks ago, the 2026 AACSB Global Standards took effect. **Standard 8, "Impact of Scholarship,"** now requires accredited schools to demonstrate that they "encourage, support, and reward the creation of intellectual contributions that have impact beyond traditional scholarly metrics." The framework is advice. Standard 8 is a requirement, and it binds the employers of most AOM members.

So the thirty-year argument about rigor and relevance has, for the first time, produced an instrument with consequences. Deans are working out compliance right now.

## The Engagement

The AOM Board of Governors has engaged your team to answer one question:

> **What should AOM change about its journals, its annual meeting, and its divisions in the next 12 months, so that an AOM member can walk into a promotion review with evidence of impact their dean will accept?**

Read that question carefully, because it fences off three things.

It is not "is impact important." That is settled, and AOM helped settle it. It is not "should schools reward impact." Standard 8 already requires them to. And it is not "what should deans do," because AOM is not a dean and cannot make them do anything.

What is left is the actual engagement: AOM has roughly 18,000 members whose employers are now on the hook for something those members cannot individually produce evidence for. AOM holds the journals they publish in, the meeting they present at, and the divisions they belong to. The Board wants to know what to do with those levers in the next 12 months, and how it would know whether any of it worked.

**The test of a good answer:** a mid-career member at a Standard 8 school could point to something AOM did and say, "that is why my dean counts this."

## The Board's Mandate (Read This Carefully)

**Rigor is the Academy's crown jewel and is not on the table.** The framework itself says rigor "remains foundational," and it prints its own dissenters at length. One survey respondent, quoted in the report, warns that broadening impact too far would move academics "away from their competitive edge … racing the discipline to the bottom in the long run." A recommendation that cannot answer that objection has not met the standard the field's own document sets.

**Explicit non-goals:**
- Lowering methodological or review standards
- "Impact-washing", relabeling existing work as impactful
- Attacking any journal, publisher, school, or person
- Restating the framework back to the Board. They co-wrote it. Assume they have read it.

**The constraint that defines this engagement: AOM is not a business school.**

The framework's central practical move is "start where you have agency," and the agency it names belongs to deans: promotion and tenure criteria, hiring, resource allocation, faculty development. AOM sets no tenure standard, hires no faculty, and allocates no research budget. Its seven-lever Ecosystem Guide and its Assessment Tool are both built around a school's mission statement and its AACSB Business School Questionnaire return. Neither instrument fits an 18,000-member association, and the framework does not say what should replace them.

What AOM does hold: six journals, the annual meeting, 26 divisions, an awards system, and convening power over the people who staff the schools now subject to Standard 8. This is an influence-strategy problem, not an authority problem.

**The white space, stated plainly.** The framework's "Call for Collective Action" gives business school leaders a dense page and accrediting bodies a full page. **Academies and Scholarly Associations get five bullets, roughly 120 words** (see `documents/research-impact-framework-2026.md`). The task force said Phase 2 is implementation. For associations, Phase 2 is a blank page. Filling it is the work.

**An open empirical question.** The Board cannot currently state its own impact baseline. The framework's Assessment Tool tells a *school* what to record. Nobody has run anything comparable at the level of a field, and AOM has no agreed measure of whether management scholarship's real-world influence is rising or falling. Part of your recommendation is proposing what AOM should count.

## Your Tasks (workshop timings)

1. **Assess (12 min).** Have Claude Code read the framework against the data room. Prompt: *"Read the 2026 research impact framework and this data room. Against the five imperatives the framework assigns to scholarly associations, where is AOM already strong, where is it weak, and what is the binding constraint?"* Then push. Which claims are grounded in a number from `data/`? Which are the AI's assumptions about an organization it does not understand?
2. **Structure (13 min).** Build a MECE issue tree of AOM's implementation options, **split by what AOM controls versus what it can only influence**. Write it to `issue-tree.md`. Getting that split right is most of the analysis.
3. **Recommend (13 min).** Draft a Pyramid-Principle one-page recommendation to the Board in `recommendation.md`: the first three moves, sequenced, each with an indicator AOM could actually measure. Stretch: have Claude Code render it as a single HTML slide.

## A Note on the 80/20

AI will read 46 pages of framework, 148 pages of accreditation standards, and a folder of literature faster than any analyst you have worked with, and it will structure a competent first draft. What it will not reliably do is notice that AOM cannot change tenure standards, that an impact ranking duplicates work AACSB has already announced for its own Phase 2, or that a proposal is politically dead on arrival with the division that would have to run it.

You know those things. Notice the moment you supply them. That is the 20%, and it is the point of the exercise.
