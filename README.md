# Engagement Brief: Academy of Management Research Impact Strategy

> **Workshop framing note:** This is a teaching case. Imagine the engagement below is real, the data is. Public sources throughout; interview notes are illustrative composites, not real individuals.

## The Client

The Academy of Management: the world's largest professional association for management scholars, roughly 18,000 members across ~120 countries, 26 divisions and interest groups, owner of the field's flagship journals (AMJ, AMR, Annals, AMP, AMD, AMLE) and of the annual meeting you are attending right now.

## The Engagement

The AOM Board of Governors has engaged your team to answer one question: **why does management research, tens of thousands of brilliant scholars producing rigorous work, have so little visible influence on how organizations are actually managed, and what should AOM do about it?**

Your data room reveals a system of interlocking incentives: promotion-and-tenure standards that reward A-journal hits over real-world impact, journals and rankings whose gatekeeping power functions like a monopoly on what "counts," and a publishing model whose intended end users, managers, have largely stopped reading.

Your job is to find the innovation opportunities hiding inside that locked system, open research infrastructure, new research products and practitioner channels, incentive redesign, and recommend to the Board where AOM should move first.

## The Board's Mandate (Read This Carefully)

**Rigor is the Academy's crown jewel and is not on the table.** The Board's view, borrowing from Akerlof (2020): our sins are of *omission*, not commission. The question is never "less rigor", it is what a rigor-only optimization leaves out, and how to pair rigor with impact.

**Explicit non-goals:**
- Lowering methodological or review standards
- "Impact-washing", relabeling existing work as impactful
- Attacking any journal, publisher, school, or person

**A constraint that matters:** AOM does not control the biggest levers. Universities set tenure standards; commercial publishers and media rankings hold much of the gatekeeping power; deans answer to accreditors. What AOM has is convening power over 18,000 scholars, ownership of the field's most prestigious journals, and the annual meeting. Treat this as an influence-strategy problem.

**An open empirical question:** the Board cannot currently tell you its impact baseline, no agreed KPI for the practical impact of management scholarship exists. Part of your diagnosis is proposing how impact should be measured at all.

## Your Tasks (workshop timings)

1. **Diagnose (12 min).** Have Claude Code read the data room. Prompt: *"Read this data room and give me the 5 key facts and your hypothesis on why impact lags."* Push back. Where is the synthesis glib? What does it miss that you, an insider, know?
2. **Structure (13 min).** Build a MECE issue tree for "How can AOM increase the real-world impact of management scholarship?" Write it to `issue-tree.md`.
3. **Communicate (13 min).** Draft a Pyramid-Principle one-page recommendation to the Board in `recommendation.md`. Stretch: have Claude Code render it as a single HTML slide.

## A Note on the 80/20

AI will do the reading, the computation, and the first-draft structuring faster than any analyst you've ever worked with. What it cannot do is know which recommendation the Board can actually adopt, which stakeholder coalition makes it stick, and which finding is naive. That 20% is you. Notice where you supply it, that's the point of the exercise.
