# Interview Notes: Business School Dean

> **Illustrative composite.** Not a real individual. Constructed to reflect positions documented in the published debate (see `documents/`). Conducted by the engagement team, 30 minutes (shortened; the dean was called into a rankings meeting).

**Role:** Dean of a top-40 US business school; former strategy scholar; 6 years in the role.

## Key Points

On incentives:
- "You want to know why my faculty write for eleven readers? Because I promote them for it. And you want to know why I promote them for it? Because every ranking that determines my applicant pool, my donor conversations, and frankly my own job counts A-hits. I'm not confused about the system. I'm compliant with it. There's a difference."
- The school's P&T document lists "impact on practice" as a criterion. Asked when it last decided a case: long pause. "It's a tiebreaker. Honestly, it's a tiebreaker that has never broken a tie."

On the market:
- Notes the paradox: executive education and specialized master's programs, where practitioners pay directly, are the school's growth engine, "and almost none of what we sell there comes out of our A-journal pipeline. My exec-ed faculty and my research stars are basically two different faculties wearing one brand."
- "Our students are the impact channel nobody counts. Forty thousand alumni running things. But show me the metric that connects an AMJ paper to what they do on Monday."

On what would change behavior:
- "If AACSB or the FT changed what they count, I'd change my scorecard the same week. If AOM built a credible impact metric, something I could defend to my provost the way I defend citation counts, I'd use it. Nobody's built it."
- Skeptical of exhortation: "Every AOM presidential address for thirty years has said 'matter more.' I've been to the talks. The talks don't move my scorecard."

## Follow-Up Call, July 2026 (15 min, after Standard 8 took effect)

We called back because the dean's earlier condition had been met: the accreditor did change what it counts.

- Aware of it, unprompted. "8.1.D. 'Encourage, support, and reward.' My associate dean has had that sentence on a slide since June."
- On what it changes: "It changes my documentation before it changes my behavior. I can evidence 'encourage' and 'support' by Friday, we fund things, we have a speaker series. 'Reward' is the word that costs money, because reward means the P&T document, and that's a faculty governance fight, not a dean's memo."
- Expects the first cycle to be compliance-shaped: "Everyone's going to write a narrative. The question nobody can answer yet is what a good one looks like, or what happens to a school that writes a weak one. Until a peer review team actually pushes back on somebody, we're all guessing."
- Still the same ask, now sharper: "The framework says assess impact with a balanced portfolio. Fine. Balanced against what? I need a benchmark. I can tell you my school's citation percentile in about four clicks. Nobody can tell me whether my impact portfolio is good, average, or embarrassing, because there's no comparison set. Somebody has to build the denominator."
- On AOM specifically, asked directly: "Honestly? I'd expect AACSB to build the tool, because it's their standard. What I'd want from AOM is the thing only AOM can do, which is get the disciplines to agree on what counts as evidence in the first place. My accounting faculty and my OB faculty do not mean the same thing by 'impact on practice.' If AOM can't settle that, nobody can."

## Read Between the Lines (engagement team notes)

The dean is rankings-trapped, not hostile, describes the equilibrium with almost clinical detachment and shows flashes of genuine frustration (the two-faculties remark). Two live leads from the first conversation: (1) the missing credible impact metric, a measurement product AOM could actually build; (2) the uncounted alumni/teaching channel, potentially the field's largest real impact pathway, currently invisible.

The follow-up sharpens both. Note the distinction the dean draws between *encourage/support* (cheap, documentable this quarter) and *reward* (expensive, requires faculty governance). Any recommendation that assumes Standard 8 will move P&T quickly is ignoring the person who would have to move it. Note also the two asks the dean makes and does not expect AACSB to fill: **a benchmark** (a denominator, so a school can tell whether its portfolio is good) and **cross-disciplinary agreement on what counts as evidence**. The second is arguably the clearest thing on the table that only an association spanning 26 divisions could do. Same coordination-problem shape as the editor interview, one level up.
