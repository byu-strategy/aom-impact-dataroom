# Interview Notes: Business School Dean

> **Illustrative composite.** Not a real individual. Constructed to reflect positions documented in the published debate (see `documents/`). Conducted by the engagement team, 30 minutes (shortened; the dean was called into a rankings meeting).

**Role:** Dean of a top-40 US business school; former strategy scholar; 6 years in the role.

## Key Points

On incentives:
- "You want to know why my faculty write for eleven readers? Because I promote them for it. And you want to know why I promote them for it? Because every ranking that determines my applicant pool, my donor conversations, and frankly my own job counts A-hits. I'm not confused about the system. I'm compliant with it. There's a difference."
- The school's P&T document lists "impact on practice" as a criterion. Asked when it last decided a case: long pause. "It's a tiebreaker. Honestly, it's a tiebreaker that has never broken a tie."

On the market:
- Notes the paradox: executive education and specialized master's programs, where practitioners pay directly, are the school's growth engine, "and almost none of what we sell there comes out of our A-journal pipeline. My exec-ed faculty and my research stars are basically two different faculties wearing one brand."
- "Our students are the impact channel nobody counts. Forty thousand alumni running things. But show me the metric that connects an AMJ paper to what they do on Monday."

On what would change behavior:
- "If AACSB or the FT changed what they count, I'd change my scorecard the same week. If AOM built a credible impact metric, something I could defend to my provost the way I defend citation counts, I'd use it. Nobody's built it."
- Skeptical of exhortation: "Every AOM presidential address for thirty years has said 'matter more.' I've been to the talks. The talks don't move my scorecard."

## Read Between the Lines (engagement team notes)

The dean is rankings-trapped, not hostile, describes the equilibrium with almost clinical detachment and shows flashes of genuine frustration (the two-faculties remark). Two live leads: (1) the missing credible impact metric, a measurement product AOM could actually build; (2) the uncounted alumni/teaching channel, potentially the field's largest real impact pathway, currently invisible. Same coordination-problem shape as the editor interview, one level up.
