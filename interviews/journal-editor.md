# Interview Notes: Senior Journal Editor

> **Illustrative composite.** Not a real individual. Constructed to reflect positions documented in the published debate (see `documents/`). Conducted by the engagement team, 45 minutes.

**Role:** Current senior editor at a top-tier management journal; 20+ years reviewing; former associate dean.

## Key Points

On the critique itself:
- "Everyone who says we should publish more 'relevant' work should sit in my chair for a month. You know what I see? I see what happens when standards slip. The replication crisis in psychology should terrify all of us. Rigor is not the problem, rigor is the only thing standing between us and being a fad industry."
- Points to the 1990s–2000s as evidence: "We chased managerial fads before, reengineering, excellence, whatever the airport bookstore was selling. The discipline earned its credibility by refusing to do that anymore."

On what the journal can and can't do:
- Acknowledges reviewers ask "what's the theoretical contribution?" far more often than "would this change what any manager does?", "That's the genre. I didn't invent it, and no single editor can change it. If I start accepting papers for practical importance and the citations don't come, our impact factor drops, the FT50 list doesn't care about my good intentions, and the deans who use my journal for tenure cases will just move to the next journal on the list."
- "I can't unilaterally disarm."

On promising directions (unprompted, near the end):
- Would support structured practitioner-implications sections "with teeth", reviewed, not boilerplate
- Interested in registered reports and open data "because that's MORE rigor, and it happens to also build trust outside"
- "If AOM as an association changed what counts, if the deans' scorecards changed, editors would follow within five years. We're downstream, not upstream."

## Read Between the Lines (engagement team notes)

The editor is not an obstacle; the editor is *trapped in an equilibrium*. Defends rigor for genuinely good reasons and privately resents being cast as the villain. The lever the editor keeps pointing at is not the journal, it's the tenure scorecard and the ranking lists upstream of the journal. Note the game-theoretic language ("unilaterally disarm"): coordination problem, not conviction problem.
