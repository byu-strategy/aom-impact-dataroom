# Interview Notes: Fifth-Year PhD Student

> **Illustrative composite.** Not a real individual. Constructed to reflect positions documented in the published debate (see `documents/`). Conducted by the engagement team, 45 minutes.

**Role:** Fifth-year doctoral candidate in organizational behavior at a top-25 program; on the job market this fall; came to the PhD from four years in industry.

## Key Points

On motivation vs. incentives:
- "I left a good job because I watched three reorgs destroy three teams and I wanted to understand why. That question is still why I'm here. It is also, and my advisor has been very clear and very kind about this, not a job-market paper."
- "The advice I've gotten, from people who genuinely want me to succeed: pick a conversation in the journals, find the gap, make a clean identification play, save the stuff you care about for after tenure. Everyone says 'after tenure.' I've started noticing how few people I can name who actually did it after tenure. The habits are the person by then."

On the rational calculus:
- Knows the math cold: one A-hit ≈ job at a research school; practitioner impact ≈ 0 on every rubric she'll face for nine years (market + 6-year clock). "I'm not a martyr. I have a partner and a kid. I'll take the deal in front of me."
- On risk: the important-question projects are slower, messier, likelier to fail review. "Akerlof would say I'm optimizing hardness over importance. Sure. He got to say that *after* the Nobel."

On what would change things:
- "If one, one, credible school listed a translated-impact product as counting toward tenure, my whole cohort would notice by Friday. We're not ideologues. We're excellent at optimizing whatever you put in front of us. That's the whole problem and the whole opportunity."
- Notes her cohort already writes threads, newsletters, and preprints "as a guilty hobby, off the books."

## Read Between the Lines (engagement team notes)

The pipeline is where the equilibrium reproduces itself: the field selects people motivated by real problems, then systematically trains the motivation into deferral. She is not cynical, she's a rational actor describing the reward function with unusual clarity. Sharpest single insight in the data room: *"We're excellent at optimizing whatever you put in front of us. That's the whole problem and the whole opportunity."* Any recommendation that changes what junior scholars can get credit for compounds for forty years; any that depends on senior scholars volunteering after tenure fights the habits objection.
